I have a Google Slides deck called **"Wall of kudos"**. Its subtitle says what it's for: _brag document for "impostor syndrome" moments_. It holds screenshots of things people have told me about my work. When my head tells me I am a fraud, I open it.

This post explains what goes in it, how I use it, and what it taught me.

## Brag document vs. wall of kudos

Julia Evans wrote about [brag documents](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/){:target="_blank"}{:rel="noopener noreferrer"}: a list of the things **you** did, so you remember them at review time. I recommend it.

[Mention that it's one of the first things I encourage to do when I start 1on1s with a new person on a company, or when mentoring]

My wall of kudos is the other half: what **other people** said. The difference matters to me. Impostor syndrome argues with my own memory. "You only think you did that well." It's harder to argue with a message someone else wrote, with their name and a date on it.

## What goes in it

Anything where someone took the time to tell me my work mattered to them:

- Messages after a talk, on LinkedIn, Mastodon, X or by email.
- The ratings and written comments from a conference.
- Notes from colleagues when I left a company.
- A Slack message from a manager about a project.
- Answers to a 360 feedback request.
- A handwritten dedication in a book.

Today it has 15 slides. <!-- TODO: check the number before publishing. -->

## My rules

1. **Screenshots, not summaries.** A summary is my version. A screenshot is theirs, with the exact words.
2. **Save it the same day.** Messages get buried in chats. I don't trust myself to find them later.
3. **Keep the source and the date.** Who, where, when. It makes the evidence concrete.
4. **Keep it private.** It's a tool for me. These are private messages, and I don't share them without asking. <!-- TODO: confirm you agree with this rule. The quotes below are anonymous. -->
5. **Include the uncomfortable parts.** When a 360 answer includes an improvement point, it stays. The deck is not a highlights reel.

## What it taught me

Reading it all in one sitting surprised me. The things people thank me for are rarely the things I would put in a brag document.

I would list technical work: pipelines, Trunk-Based Development, Continuous Deployment. People mention that too. A team lead wrote that my talk on Trunk-Based Development was one of their main inspirations when they took over their team, and that they stopped considering other ways of working. A release manager wrote about the "baby steps" approach on a project:

> The wording might sound cute... but let me assure you that the effect is that of a giant.

But most of the messages are about **how** I work with people:

- _"You tend to focus on what actually matters and move things forward instead of overcomplicating stuff."_
- _"I also like how much you share: context, thoughts, ideas. It creates a feeling of openness and makes it easier for everyone to learn from each other."_
- _"Thank you for being an open and honest advocate for mental health and humanity in general."_
- _"I think you made a difference in our work environment."_

None of this would appear in my own list. I don't track "shared context today" or "talked openly about mental health". That is the point of the wall: it shows me the parts of my work that I can't see myself.

## How to start yours

1. Create an empty document today. Slides work well for screenshots.
2. Search your chats and email for "thank you", "gracias" or "kudos". Screenshot what you find.
3. From now on, save new messages the day you get them.

It takes ten minutes. The next time impostor syndrome shows up, you will have evidence ready.
